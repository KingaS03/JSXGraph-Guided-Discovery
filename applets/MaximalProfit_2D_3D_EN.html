Profit Maximisation in Two and Three Dimensions

The two sliders for \(t\) are synchronised. If you change the tax value in one visualisation, it is automatically updated in the other visualisation as well.

1. Profit Maximisation with a Per-Unit Tax – 2D

For each fixed tax value \(t\), the firm chooses the quantity \(Q\). Here, \(Q\) is the decision variable and \(t\) is a parameter: changing \(t\) changes the entire profit function.

Model and Optimum

\[ \pi_t(Q)=-\frac{1}{600}Q^3+(50-t)Q-\frac{1000}{3}, \qquad Q\in[0,300]. \]

For an interior optimum, \(\pi_t'(Q)=0\) gives the quantity \(Q^*(t)=\sqrt{200(50-t)}\). For \(t\ge 50\), however, the constrained maximum occurs at the boundary, where \(Q^*(t)=0\). The corresponding maximal profit is \(\pi_t\!\left(Q^*(t)\right)\).

The applet represents these relationships in different coordinate systems. Therefore, always pay attention to which quantities are shown on the axes.

Explore:

  1. Change the tax value \(t\). How do the maximum of the blue profit function, the optimal quantity \(Q^*(t)\), and the maximal profit \(\pi_t(Q^*(t))\) change?
  2. Use the second slider to increase the number \(n\) of additional tax values displayed. Which curve do the corresponding maximum points appear to form?
  3. Display the curve of optimal points. What do its coordinates \(\bigl(Q^*(t),\pi_t(Q^*(t))\bigr)\) represent? Why is this curve not yet the graph of maximal profit as a function of \(t\)?
  4. Next, display \(Q^*(t)\) and \(\pi_t(Q^*(t))\) as functions of \(t\). What differs from the green curve? In particular, compare the quantities on the horizontal axes.
  5. Explore values of \(t\) close to and above \(50\). Why does the maximum occur at \(Q=0\), even though the first-order condition no longer yields an interior optimum?

2. The Profit Function as a Surface in \((t,Q,\pi)\)-Space – 3D

In the two-dimensional visualisation, a separate profit function in terms of \(Q\) is drawn for each fixed tax value \(t\). Here, all these graphs are combined into a surface in three-dimensional space.

Model and Optimal Points

\[ \pi_t(Q)=-\frac{1}{600}Q^3+(50-t)Q-\frac{1000}{3}, \qquad Q\in[0,300]. \]

Over the range \(0\le t\le 60\) considered here, \[ Q^*(t)= \begin{cases} \sqrt{200(50-t)}, & 0\le t<50,\\ 0, & 50\le t\le60. \end{cases} \] The optimal points form the space curve \(\bigl(t,Q^*(t),\pi_t(Q^*(t))\bigr)\). For a clearer visualisation, the values of \(Q\) and \(\pi\) are scaled in the three-dimensional coordinate system.

The blue curve is the intersection of the surface with the plane corresponding to a fixed tax value. The red point marks the maximum of this cross-section.

  2. Display the curve of optimal points. What do the three coordinates of a point on this space curve represent?
  3. Project the optimal curve onto the \(Q\pi\)-plane and compare it with the green curve in the 2D applet. What information about \(t\) is lost in this projection?
  4. Project the curve onto the \(tQ\)- and \(t\pi\)-planes. Which projection represents \(Q^*(t)\), and which represents the maximal profit \(\pi_t(Q^*(t))\)?
  5. Explain how the three planar representations arise from the same space curve and which question is best answered by each projection.
